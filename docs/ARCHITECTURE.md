# Simulation and rendering

## Two clocks

`src/simulation.ts` is engine-independent. A positive fixed 1/60-second step advances player gravity, damage cooldowns, transitions and physical coins. Player movement is 121 source pixels/second, gravity 680, and full-held jump height 56. Each landing allows one extra airborne jump on a fresh press; both launches reset vertical velocity and their variable-height energy budget. Releasing jump caps rise between half and full height. Ballistic rise excludes support lift, maintaining a 2:1 envelope as tuning changes. Holding never repeats; branch/merge alone never restores the air jump. Keyboard/touch ownership is aggregated, with an explicit fresh edge preserving release/repress between fixed ticks.

First/second jump bursts and the sparse trail capture absolute world-space emission records without branch ownership. Their seeded analytic motion uses forward action time, survives removed supports, and freezes on pause. Independent live tuning has zero/off paths; reduced effects suppresses emission and drawing. Expired records are pruned, with at most 128 live cosmetic particles. They render as whole-pixel rectangles inside the existing native composition and feed motion fringes from their actual world poses.

Environmental time changes by **actual controlled horizontal displacement / configured movement speed**. An obstructed player does not advance it. Patrol positions, spinner angles, trap phases and projectile trajectories are analytical functions of that signed clock. There is no negative physics timestep. Falling can collide with a stationary hazard.

Pause, focus loss and insufficient display space suspend simulation. Inputs are cleared; focus restoration does not automatically resume. Rendering the world does not mutate its temporal state.

The player-specific animation is forward-only, following the supplied animation contract. The 130x135 sheet contains untrimmed 26x27 cells selected by labeled frame names, not inferred alpha bounds or exported tag ranges:

| Clip | Frames | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle | 5, first row | 240 ms |
| Walk | 5, second row | 50 ms |
| Jump_Up | 4, third row | One launch draw, then 50 ms; loop last two |
| JumpDown | 4, fourth row | Restart at apex; 50 ms, loop last two |
| Land | First cell, fifth row | At least one draw, then 60 ms |

The last nonzero movement input controls facing; left movement mirrors rather than reversing pose order. The renderer advances animation once per draw and guards the first launch/landing frames even if several physics steps occur. A consumed launch frame advances only at the next unpaused update, so pausing immediately after either jump retains the pose already drawn.

## Branches, terrain and irreversible transactions

Branches have stable instance IDs and sparse staged/converted sets. Encounter IDs identify lineage; reward keys combine the owner branch ID and encounter ID. Converted ancestry is cached by earliest active depth. Branching has no cost or arbitrary depth cap.

The stack is iterative, not recursive object cloning. The highest fork replaces every enemy, including shooters, with a switch at the ordinary 15-pixel height. Every unconverted lower copy remains active, including main; main-only play never replaces enemies with switches. Forking enables the former top shooter as a lower source. Merging makes the receiving parent switch-only if it remains above main; neither visibility change itself converts the source. Merging converts only staged targets in the receiving parent and carries staged relationships down. Repeated stages/merges cannot repeat a reward event.

One unit is 24 world pixels; branch lanes are 58 pixels apart. The fork is placed exactly 24 pixels behind the player. `surfaceAt` evaluates the diagonal between the commit and its right-hand endpoint, then the child lane. Walking follows that support in both directions; airborne motion uses world height and lands on the same surface. Crossing left of a fork follows the parent path without deleting the branch. The child ramp remains physically present throughout its collapse and is removed only at settlement; no engine colliders accumulate.

Lift and collapse have independent durations, initially 0.34 and 0.2 seconds. `surfaceAt` and the cached contact-height arrays share the same `surfaceHeight` transform: cubic ease-out lifting from the parent surface, power-4.7 ease-in collapsing into it. The top platform, ramp endpoints, enemies, switches, coins and player support all use this geometry. The jump offset and vertical velocity continue forward within the moving support frame, including airborne operations. These are forward-only physical lifts, not teleports or damage shields. Swept contact checks use the player's **actual world-space body** across relevant branch copies, including during transitions. Gates clamp genuine horizontal entry when the body overlaps their world height. A blocked lift may continue vertically while horizontal time remains frozen. Contact/projectile damage grants 1.35 seconds of post-hit protection; branch operations themselves grant none.

Only the topmost reached lane can stage; collection and staging wait until the lift/collapse finishes. Merge acceptance leaves targets/rewards pending. Each target converts once when the actual descending support reaches its receiving collider top; its pop, burst and coins occur on that impact. Conversion precedes the same step's contact checks, preventing a staged target from damaging the player after impact without exempting unstaged hazards. Geometry, branch identity and source shots remain until settlement; cleanup and merge audio happen at completion. An uninterrupted held Merge serializes one collapse at a time, immediately after the previous tween finishes by default. `holdDelay` is an optional extra post-tween delay, never a competing duration. Release/cancel, focus loss, pause, restart and new branching end the combo; an already accepted tween finishes normally. Each completed held merge raises pitch, and the fourth gives one flourish. Main stops descent.

Staging immediately identifies corresponding copies through every ancestor. Original activated atlas variants swap purple accents for cyan/white without changing alpha, source pixels or red danger details. Pixel brackets and links supplement color; activated enemies remain dangerous until converted. Unrelated lineages do not change. Coins on a collapsed branch transfer to its parent and retain IDs, positions and velocities. Source destruction, switch-only status and removal preserve only already-airborne flight until its next temporal boundary.

Coin launches use deterministic per-copy impulses. Forward-clock gravity, surface-normal restitution, friction and nearby circle separation make piles spread. Positions are relative to authoritative moving support; crossing left of a ramp transfers ownership onto the reached parent. This keeps backtracked coins visible and collectible. Idle highlights are native pixel strokes; reduced effects restrain shimmer. Coin motion only feeds cosmetic fringes, never the reverse environmental clock.

## Reversible shots and bounded work

Shots are identified by **shooter branch instance / encounter / firing-event index**. A relevant living shooter emits only when forward environmental motion reaches its firing event. Frozen time, newly copied branches and first exposure never backfill historical shots from current geometry. The emission samples the shared eased support at the event's fractional fixed-step age, and aims toward the player's interpolated event position. Unseen historical shots are not fabricated on reverse traversal.

A shot travels at 128 pixels/second for 2.8 environmental seconds. Its world-space origin and velocity stay fixed while airborne, even when its source keeps lifting or collapsing. Living-source history reverses across expiry and back to its recorded birth location. On a subsequent forward birth crossing, the same event ID and aim are reused, but the origin is resampled from the now-current eased muzzle. Thus action-clock geometry cannot drag airborne shots or make a living copy fire from an obsolete support.

Conversion immediately retires inactive source history. A shot already strictly past birth and before expiry may finish its current flight, but permanently retires on reaching birth or expiry; later rewind cannot revive it. Retirement removes it from the spatial index and applies equally to rendering and swept collision. The record remains in the journal for identity/history, not as permission to replay. Receiving-parent and inherited child conversions retire their own sources without suppressing living counterparts on lower branches.

The activity predicate gates bodies and new firing events alike. A newly absent top source also retires inactive replay history while allowing current airborne flight to finish at its next birth/expiry boundary. If that living copy later becomes a lower source, only a genuine forward firing-event crossing may reuse its retired event ID. Converted sources can never do so. Removed-copy airborne records detach from branch indexes and leave the journal after retirement; no reused depth can become their owner.

Trajectory bounding boxes are indexed in 128x128 world cells. Collision and drawing query nearby buckets, not every historical shot. Shooter relevance uses finite attack range, including sources outside the viewport. Per-step shared height arrays avoid repeatedly walking every ancestor for each shot or contact query.

## Endless state and limits

Seeded 480-pixel chunks produce deterministic encounters on re-entry. Independent weighted gap allocation creates tighter groups and breathing room, not jitter around a regular grid. Leading/trailing margins and swept hazard widths (including patrol range) constrain placement; excessive widths reduce encounter count. The first chunk retains a fair learning stretch. Spacing/type/period difficulty increases with forward chunk progress, not frame count or repeated traversal. Distance rewards use the maximum reached X. Chunk definitions have a 24-entry cache and can be regenerated without changing copy identities.

Visible enemies are pooled. Rendering visits the camera's nearby branch lanes and spatially relevant coins/shots, with a fixed logical viewport. Coin simulation uses three local chunk queues, rotating at most 32 coins per chunk per step. Two collision passes inspect at most 16 neighbors per selected coin through branch/X cells. Thus at most 96 coins integrate and 3,072 neighbor entries are inspected per step, regardless of retained rewards. Resting floor coins skip integration; distant coins sleep and resume on return. Extremely large piles are time-sliced rather than silently deleted or granted a global reward cap.

Exploration retains shot launch history, uncollected coins and permanent reward/branch state. This grows with explored content and branch copies. Infinite play on finite hardware is **not constant-memory**; 8/64/256-depth tests are stress cases, not caps. The benchmark reports retained growth separately from local query counts.

## Pixel composition and camera

Every world sprite uses one native texel per world pixel and the same camera transform. Enemies are authored on the character's native grid; spinner orientations are pre-rasterized, not smoothly rotated over pixel sprites. Graph strokes use integer source coordinates and whole-pixel thickness. Optional CRT glow is a separate monitor-space optical treatment, not higher-resolution world geometry.

`src/pixel.ts` installs an actual Phaser 4 **internal GameObject Filter** on the native world composition, before camera zoom. Sprites, geometry, links and particles first rasterize at one source texel per world pixel. The filter samples these native pixel centers and applies the ten-color palette. Its result is baked to `presentation`, then transformed once by the camera into the physical output buffer. The world targets start at 1008x572 and grow only when a lower configured zoom needs more coverage (up to 1608x908 at 0.3); they never downsample sprites to a fixed 480x270 post-zoom world first.

After palette filtering, bounded optional channel fringes use real object poses in native world pixels. Stable platform anchors and separate actual connector endpoints exclude camera displacement; monotonic action time retains motion between fixed ticks at high refresh rates. Camera projection follows, then the HUD and a static monitor-space CRT filter. Optical separation interpolates neighboring samples along the optical ray so subpixel settings do not disappear through nearest-neighbor rounding. This does not change the native world texture or its palette/zoom projection. Four cached shader variants separate narrow/wide optics and glow: the narrow path uses seven texture samples, while combined wide effects use at most seventeen. The selected preset and viewport determine the variant; stronger defaults can select the wider path. Wide glow uses a fixed, bounded twelve-tap kernel; ordinary small-radius glow uses four taps. Normal-mode values are not silently capped below the exposed tuning ranges.

Motion is independently disabled by reduced effects or pause. CRT uses explicit reduced-effects caps and bypasses buffers above its 4096-dimension/4K-area budget, never by resampling. Recovery or disabling CRT clears only the CRT-owned budget notice, preserving unrelated warnings. DOM menus remain unfiltered and usable even at extreme canvas treatment.

The ink entry is `#08060f`, shared with Phaser clear color; outer DOM, fullscreen backdrop and letterbox surfaces are pure black. Original `#14102b` sprite ink explicitly maps to it before palette selection; foreground colors remain unchanged. Disabling optional effects restores exact palette-grid output.

The physical output is 480x270 multiplied by an integer presentation scale derived from available space and DPR. HUD geometry is separate, in stable logical UI space; DOM text uses the original self-hosted Fork Pixel font. A world texel spans `camera.zoom * physicalPixelScale` physical display pixels. Below one physical pixel per texel, the display necessarily loses detail; this is distinct from an additional shader-induced resampling. Native world patches remain unchanged during zoom/pan. Final sampling admits GPU texture-coordinate edge ties, not blurred interpolation. A too-small viewport pauses and explains the limitation.

Graph support is the upper edge of a two-row bright band. The commit and connector centerline meet at `(forkX, -surfaceHeight + 1)` and terminate at `(forkX + rampLength, -childSurfaceHeight + 1)`. Per-column coverage samples the shared surface up to, but not beyond, the landing column; it does not extrapolate a high endpoint. Parent lanes remain continuous beneath main and nested ramps throughout the tween.

`src/camera.ts` starts at 0.88 world scale, damps the deadzone and directional look-ahead, and reduces zoom with branch depth to a 0.48 readability floor. The player is free to move within the deadzone rather than staying pinned to one screen coordinate. Branch visibility stays local once the zoom floor is reached. World coordinates rasterize on a shared integer source origin. Camera motion remains continuous until its final physical origin is rounded; shake uses whole world pixels, and physics retain subpixel coordinates.

`window.forkingHell.snapshot()`, `.diagnostics()` and `.projectiles()` provide read-only observations: actual animation state, world scales, camera origin, shader-pass count, effective CRT uniforms/status, retained/visible counts, nearby source muzzles and active shot trajectories. They do not expose game mutation or persistence APIs.

## Tuning and UI input

`src/defaults.json` is the complete versioned tuning document. `src/tuning.ts` defines the mapped numeric type, bounds, exact-key parser and restart classification; `src/tuning-panel.ts` is a semantic, nonmodal DOM editor. See [Tuning](TUNING.md) for the JSON workflow and integration contract.

Ordinary intro keys start only after assets and the viewport are ready, with Tune and blocking recovery dialogs closed. The starting event is consumed; unowned held-key repeats cannot become gameplay input. A single document-level router handles menu arrows, modal Tab order, Escape, checkbox Enter activation and intro admission before `Controls` receives gameplay events. Shortcuts, composition and native editing pass through. Notices join a blocking dialog's focus cycle without stealing focus when announced, and dismissal restores the previous valid owner. Tune closes back to the underlying dialog when paused or undersized; otherwise it returns to its toggle.

Menu focus owns its keys and clears gameplay input. Pause/results trap Tab, and Up/Down traverse their options; native checkbox, number and range behavior remains intact. Tune focuses its first summary, supports Space/Enter and Escape, and permits Tab back to the game. The desktop diagram uses the mockup's Fork / Left-Merge-Right / Jump arrangement; native touch targets retain their two-handed layout and share original grid-aligned SVG icons. Keycap and touch pressed states share `Controls` state. Page selection/callouts are suppressed except in editable fields; gameplay buttons retain pointer capture and cancellation.
